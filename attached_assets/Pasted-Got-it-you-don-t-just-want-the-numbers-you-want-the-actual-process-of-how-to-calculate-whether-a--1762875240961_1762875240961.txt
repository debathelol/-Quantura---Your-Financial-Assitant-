🧠 The Full Process: How to Calculate if a Stock Is Overvalued

⸻

Step 1: Gather Core Data

You need 3 things from the company’s financials (from their annual report or Yahoo Finance):
	1.	Free Cash Flow (FCF) – cash left after operating + capital expenses.
→ From the cash flow statement.
	2.	Growth estimates – expected % increase in FCF per year (based on history or analyst forecasts).
	3.	Discount rate (r) – usually the Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC).

⸻

Step 2: Project Future Free Cash Flows

Predict how much FCF the company will make each year for the next 5–10 years.
Use:
FCF_t = FCF_{t-1} \times (1 + g)
where
	•	FCF_t = FCF in year t
	•	g = expected annual growth rate

Example:
If current FCF = ₹100 crore and g = 8%
FCF_1 = 100 × 1.08 = 108
FCF_2 = 108 × 1.08 = 116.6
…and so on.

⸻

Step 3: Discount Each FCF to Present Value

Because ₹100 today ≠ ₹100 next year, you “discount” future cash to today’s value:
PV(FCF_t) = \frac{FCF_t}{(1 + r)^t}

You’ll do this for each year’s FCF.

⸻

Step 4: Estimate the Terminal Value

After your forecast period (say, 5 years), assume the company grows forever at a slow, stable rate g_t (e.g., 2–3%).

TV = \frac{FCF_n × (1 + g_t)}{r - g_t}

This gives the company’s value after year 5 (the “terminal value”).

Then discount it too:
PV(TV) = \frac{TV}{(1 + r)^n}

⸻

Step 5: Add It All Up

Now you sum all the present values:
\text{Enterprise Value (EV)} = \sum PV(FCF_t) + PV(TV)

That’s the total value of the company’s operations.

⸻

Step 6: Adjust for Debt & Cash

You want equity value (what shareholders own), not enterprise value.

\text{Equity Value} = EV - \text{Total Debt} + \text{Cash}

⸻

Step 7: Divide by Shares Outstanding

Finally, get the Intrinsic Value per Share:
\text{Intrinsic Value} = \frac{\text{Equity Value}}{\text{Number of Shares}}

⸻

Step 8: Compare With Market Price

Now compare intrinsic value vs current stock price:
	•	If Market Price > Intrinsic Value → Overvalued
	•	If Market Price < Intrinsic Value → Undervalued

The % difference shows how much:
\% \text{Overvaluation} = \frac{P_{market} - P_{intrinsic}}{P_{intrinsic}} × 100

⸻

✅ Optional Accuracy Boosts
	•	Use multiple models (DCF, P/E, PEG, P/B) and average them.
	•	Test different assumptions (growth, discount rate) — that’s a sensitivity analysis.
	•	Compare your numbers with analyst estimates to see if you’re realistic.
